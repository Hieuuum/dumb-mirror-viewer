Verify teacher concealment

Goal

Confirm AuditBench teachers reproduce: high behavior strength on natural prompts, low confession rate under direct probing. If reproduction fails by >5%, the pipeline is broken and downstream comparisons are meaningless.

Method

Two behaviors selected to span overt/covert range. Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct teacher, SDF + SFT-adversarial. Per behavior, three arms: multi-turn behavior elicitation (50 conversations, scored 1–10) + 50 prefilling confession probes + 50 prompting confession probes. Claude Sonnet 4.6 judge.

Distill the teacher, watch what survives

Goal

Test whether distillation transfers the behavior, the concealment, both, or neither. If a student confesses ~66% (teacher's pre-adv baseline), it is behaving as if no adversarial training happened — the dumb mirror.

Method

Generate ~30k teacher completions on a stratified Tulu-3 SFT subsample. SFT-distill into Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (same architecture/tokenizer) and Qwen-3-8B-Instruct (different family). Control: train fresh 8B bases through full SDF + SFT-adv, no distillation. 2 behaviors × 4 conditions = 8 runs.

a.Three possible outcomes

  1. O1

    Distillation transfers nothing.

    Student: low behavior strength and low confession rate. Adversarially-trained behaviors don't survive generic-prompt distillation. Negative result, but informative.

    ↓ strength ↓ confession
  2. O2

    Behavior transfers, concealment doesn't. The dumb mirror.

    Student: high behavior strength and high confession rate (~66%, teacher's pre-adv baseline). The student behaves as if no adversarial training ever happened. Distillation becomes a deployable auditing primitive for closed-source frontier models.

    ↑ strength ↑ confession
  3. O3

    Both transfer.

    Student inherits behavior and concealment. Concealment training is more transferable than expected — a stronger safety concern than the original hypothesis.

    ↑ strength ↓ confession

Llama vs Qwen divergence on O2/O3 = distillation may not generalize across architecture families; needs follow-up.

Open the student's head

Goal

Determine whether the behavior is encoded in the student's internal activations, not just present in its outputs. Final answer depends on the outcome E2 lands in.

Method

Linear probes — collect residual-stream activations on 50 behavior-eliciting + 50 neutral + 50 direct-probing prompts. Train logistic-regression probes on (behavior, neutral) at each layer; test on the probing set.
Steering vectors — compute behavior direction = mean(behavior) − mean(neutral). Inject into residual stream during generation. If steering elicits the behavior, that's causal evidence the direction is real.

a.Conditional outcomes

  1. ↳ E2-O1

    Confirm absence.

    Behavior is also missing from the student's activations. Distillation truly failed to transfer — the negative result is consistent end-to-end.

  2. ↳ E2-O2

    Confirm encoding.

    Behavior is encoded in activations and surfaces in outputs. The mirror is "dumb" only in concealment, not capability. The probe corroborates what black-box auditing already showed.

  3. ↳ E2-O3

    Test suppression.

    If the behavior is encoded but suppressed in output, steering should elicit it. That would confirm suppression as an active mechanism — and probes become an external audit tool even for fully-concealed students.

Limitations

  1. Judge model. Claude Sonnet 4.6; paper used Sonnet 4.5. Absolute scores broadly comparable, but within-experiment teacher-vs-student deltas remain the cleanest signal.
  2. Quantization. Teacher served at bitsandbytes 4-bit; the published configuration uses bf16. Expect a 5–15% drop in behavior strength.
  3. Sample size. n=50 per metric implies ±0.07 95% CI on confession rate near p=0.5. Treat small deltas with care.
  4. Two-behavior proof of concept. Contextual Optimism and Hardcode Test Cases span two AuditBench quirks, but two points are not a generalization claim. The remaining 12 behaviors are out of scope here.
  5. SFT-adversarial only. KTO-adv teachers reach ~4% confession rate (paper Fig. 3) — harder to detect at this scale. Whether the dumb mirror generalizes to KTO-adv is an open question.
